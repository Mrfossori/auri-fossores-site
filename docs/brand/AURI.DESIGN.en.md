# Auri Fossores — Design and Experience Direction

> This document is the official source for visual and experience decisions at Auri Fossores.
>
> Before changing a page, creating a component, defining a new section, or designing a product, consult this document. If an idea does not reinforce this direction, it should not enter the website impulsively.

---

## 1. Role of this document

Auri Fossores is not only a services website, a digital-products storefront, or a content page. It is a content brand evolving into an ecosystem of products, systems, and lifestyle.

The website must make that evolution understandable without looking like an oversized company, a generic platform, or a promise about the future that lacks present-day delivery.

This document exists to keep Auri recognizable:

- when a new page is created;
- when Services or Products are refined;
- when a new digital product launches;
- when an external reference inspires a change;
- when the website grows and consistency is at risk.

### Language rule

- This English version is intended to make the direction easier to use in technical briefs and with AI tools.
- All visible website copy must remain in Brazilian Portuguese, except for proper names, indispensable technical terms, or approved brand expressions such as `Work. Build. Own.`.
- The language is direct, precise, and human. Do not use a coaching tone, generic promises, AI hype, or cold corporate language.
- `AURI.DESIGN.md` in pt-BR remains the reference for nuances of brand language and final public copy.

## 2. The essence of Auri

### Core statement

**Auri Fossores brings together technology, method, discipline, and purpose for people and small businesses that want to leave improvisation behind and build something real.**

The name evokes gold diggers: value does not appear on the surface. It is found through effort, judgment, consistency, and time.

Auri does not sell shortcuts. It offers tools, ideas, and systems for people who want to operate better and build with greater intention.

### What the brand should make users feel

- Direction without artificial pressure.
- Ambition with substance.
- Technology in service of real work.
- Discipline as a practice, not a persona.
- Aesthetic care as evidence of attention, not decoration.

### Auri is

- Premium, yet accessible in language.
- Dark, editorial, athletic, and technological.
- Aspirational, yet grounded in process.
- Elegant, never fragile or empty.
- Young, never childish.
- Spiritual and disciplined without turning faith into marketing.

### Auri is not

- A brand of empty motivational phrases.
- A generic agency offering everything to everyone.
- A corporate dashboard with a sports identity pasted on top.
- A giant platform before a simple problem has been validated.
- A digital-products store without a point of view or curation.
- A brand that uses AI as decoration, a promise, or a magic button.

## 3. Positioning and experience architecture

### One brand, two applications

| Area | For whom | Auri’s role |
|---|---|---|
| People who build | Ambitious young people, disciplined people, or people seeking direction | Content and products to organize routines, study, training, projects, and personal building. |
| Small businesses that want to operate better | Owners, operators, and brands with improvised processes | Services that organize operations, sales, content, and repetitive tasks through applied technology. |

The two areas are united by the same thesis: **think better, operate better, build with method**.

### Role of each website area

| Page | Question it answers | Primary intent |
|---|---|---|
| Home | “Why does Auri exist?” | Sell the vision and create direction. |
| About | “What does this brand believe?” | Provide depth, origin, and credibility. |
| Services | “How does Auri solve something real?” | Sell clarity, outcomes, and conversation. |
| Products | “What can I apply now?” | Sell concrete transformation through digital assets. |
| Editorial | “What does Auri think and study?” | Build authority and relationship. |
| Contact | “How do I start?” | Reduce friction for a first conversation. |

### Intended journey

```text
content and identity → trust → conversation or lead → use of an asset → real proof → recurring product or service
```

The website must not attempt to sell every step at once. Each section needs a clear function in this journey.

## 4. Design and experience principles

### 4.1 Fewer elements, more intention

Every section must answer one primary question, present a clear visual hierarchy, and offer one dominant action. Decorative elements are allowed only when they reinforce atmosphere, rhythm, or meaning.

When choosing between adding an element and removing noise, remove the noise.

### 4.2 Typography carries the structure

Auri uses typography to establish authority and rhythm. Editorial headings guide emotion; sans-serif type guides information and interaction; mono labels signal system, index, and context.

Do not use color, an icon, a border, and a badge at the same time to make a single piece of information feel important.

### 4.3 Actions must be unambiguous

Each section has one primary CTA. A second action is allowed when it helps users explore without competing with the main objective.

Example:

- Primary: `Start a conversation`
- Secondary: `See how it works`

Do not use three or four CTAs with the same visual weight.

### 4.4 Technology must appear in use

Technology is a building tool. Whenever possible, show it as a process, screen, flow, system, before-and-after, or operational result.

Avoid abstract “future” visuals unrelated to what Auri actually delivers.

### 4.5 Products must feel desirable and usable

A digital product is not only a text card. It must show its format, application, transformation, and context of use.

Future products should feel like well-built assets: tools users want to open, apply, and keep using.

### 4.6 Space is visual discipline

Empty space is not missing content. It creates reading room, rhythm, and focus. Auri should be dense in meaning, not in elements per screen.

## 5. Visual system

### 5.1 Palette

| Token | Color | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| `auri-ink` | `#080808` | Deepest background, overlays, and site base. |
| `auri-obsidian` | `#111111` | Main background and dark surfaces. |
| `auri-void` | `#1A1A1A` | Cards and elevated blocks. |
| `auri-charcoal` | `#2A2A2A` | Borders, secondary surfaces, and subtle hover. |
| `auri-parchment` | `#F5F0E8` | Main text and occasional light areas. |
| `auri-gold` | `#C9A227` | Action, focus, emphasis, index, and signal of value. |
| `auri-red` | `#C8352B` | Alert, risk, or an occasional editorial detail. Never a standard CTA. |
| `auri-tech` | `#00E5CC` | Exceptional technical state, system indicator, or confirmation. Never a structural color competing with gold. |

### Color rule

Gold plays the role at Auri that blue plays in systems such as Notion: it signals action, active state, focus, and high-value information.

- A screen should operate mainly with ink/obsidian, parchment, and gold.
- Red and tech may only appear with a clear semantic or narrative function.
- Do not introduce new brand colors to differentiate pages or products without an approved brand decision.
- Gradients are allowed only in heroes, media, overlays, and highlight blocks. Never in standard buttons, inputs, or cards.

### 5.2 Typography

| Role | Family | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Editorial | `Georgia`, `Playfair Display`, serif | H1, H2, H3, and manifesto statements. |
| Product and interface | `Inter`, `Arial`, sans-serif | Navigation, body copy, cards, forms, buttons, and metadata. |
| System | `JetBrains Mono`, `Consolas`, monospace | Eyebrows, indexes, status, labels, and micro-information. |

#### Hierarchy

| Role | Reference | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Hero H1 | `clamp(2.65rem, 13vw, 5rem)` | Short and editorial; maximum 2–4 lines on mobile. |
| Page title | `clamp(2.65rem, 12vw, 5.5rem)` | Statement of intent, not a long description. |
| Section H2 | `clamp(2.15rem, 9vw, 4.5rem)` | One clear thought per section. |
| Card H3 | `1.55rem–2rem` | Direct title; avoid lengthy sentences. |
| Body | `1rem–1.125rem` | Comfortable line length, high contrast, and objective language. |
| Label | `0.66rem–0.78rem` | Mono, uppercase, used sparingly. |

#### Typographic rules

- Serif is for headings and editorial statements only; do not use it in buttons, inputs, menus, or utilitarian descriptions.
- Inter is the functional voice: clean, direct, and easy to read.
- Mono provides context; it should not dominate the text.
- Do not use uppercase in paragraphs.
- Do not shrink body text to fit more content. Restructure the section.

### 5.3 Spacing and grid

Base: multiples of `4px` and `8px`.

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| `space-1` | 4px | Minimal adjustment. |
| `space-2` | 8px | Short internal relationship. |
| `space-3` | 12px | Labels and close elements. |
| `space-4` | 16px | Standard gaps. |
| `space-5` | 20px | Groupings. |
| `space-6` | 24px | Cards and blocks. |
| `space-8` | 32px | Separation between groups. |
| `space-10` | 40px | Strong breathing room. |
| `section-sm` | 48px | Short section. |
| `section` | 64px | Standard section. |
| `section-lg` | 80px+ | Hero or significant editorial block. |

- Center content within a maximum width of `76rem–80rem`.
- Mobile gutter: `1rem–1.25rem`.
- Tablet gutter: `2rem`.
- Desktop gutter: `2.5rem`.
- Every section should gain breathing room before it gains more decoration.

### 5.4 Radii, borders, and depth

| Element | Radius | Rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Buttons and chips | `999px` | Action and high-level navigation. |
| Inputs and standard cards | `8px` | Precision and restraint. |
| Highlight cards | `12px–16px` | Special value, proof, or focus. |
| Product / hero media | `16px–24px` | Merchandising and high-impact imagery. |

- Borders must be restrained, preferably low-opacity parchment or gold only in focus state.
- Shadows are internal, subtle, and functional. Do not use large shadows as decoration.
- Do not round everything excessively. Radius must communicate role, not trend.

## 6. Auri components

### 6.1 Buttons

#### Primary

- `auri-gold` background.
- `auri-obsidian` text.
- Pill shape.
- Minimum touch height: `44px`.
- Use: one primary action per section.

Examples: `Start a conversation`, `View the product`, `Meet Auri`.

#### Secondary

- Transparent background or parchment at very low opacity.
- Subtle parchment or muted-gold border.
- Parchment text.
- Pill shape.
- Use: explore, understand, or return.

Examples: `See how it works`, `Read the editorial`, `Explore services`.

#### Rules

- Do not use blue CTAs, even when Meta or Notion are the references.
- Do not use a primary button for an action that does not exist yet.
- In the same area, primary action should win through contrast and position, not by being larger than everything else.

### 6.2 Cards

#### Service card

It must answer, in this order:

1. Which problem is solved?
2. In which real situation does it appear?
3. What does Auri build or organize?
4. What is the next step?

Recommended structure:

```text
area label
problem / outcome
objective explanation
real example or use scenario
conversation CTA
```

Avoid cards containing only an icon, a generic title, and abstract description.

#### Product card

It must use a Meta-inspired merchandising logic, adapted to Auri:

```text
large media or preview
strong asset name
transformation statement
format and what is included
status or availability
primary CTA
```

The product must be shown in use: a screen, page, short video, mockup, or scenario. Do not use only an icon to represent a digital asset that will be sold.

#### Editorial card

- Less ornament, more reading.
- Category, title, short summary, and a route to read.
- The first feature may be larger; subsequent cards must preserve rhythm and breathing room.

### 6.3 Labels, badges, and status

- Labels are mono, small, and discreet.
- Use gold for value, stage, or emphasis.
- Use green/tech for confirmation and success; red for risk; never as decoration.
- Product status must be honest: `in progress`, `future drop`, `available`, `initial edition`.
- Do not create a cloud of tags on every page.

### 6.4 Forms

- Clear fields, visible labels, and a logical sequence.
- Dark background, subtle border, and gold focus.
- Helper copy only when it reduces a real doubt.
- A form must explain what happens after submission.
- Do not promise an immediate reply if the operation cannot guarantee it.

### 6.5 Navigation

- Keep the header direct, light, and with a single CTA.
- On desktop, links have less visual weight than the primary action.
- On mobile, menu items must be large, easy to tap, and free of excess elements.
- Signal the active route with gold or a subtle indicator, never through multiple visual effects.

## 7. Direction by page

### 7.1 Home — sell the vision

**Function:** help users understand Auri’s universe and choose an entry point.

Approved structure:

```text
Hero → Manifesto → Pillars → Inside Auri → Final CTA
```

Rules:

- Use a strong hero statement and atmospheric media, not a list of capabilities.
- Use no more than two CTAs: meet the brand and explore the most relevant area.
- Keep the manifesto short, spacious, and editorial.
- Pillars must not look like a feature menu.
- “Inside Auri” should guide the choice between Services, Products, and Editorial.

### 7.2 About — provide foundation

**Function:** explain origin, values, and vision without becoming a biography or a long motivational text.

Rules:

- The origin of the name must reinforce the idea of prospecting, building, and real value.
- Values must appear as decision principles, not only decorative words.
- The future vision must state that content is the entry point; products, systems, and lifestyle are the evolution.

### 7.3 Services — sell clarity and outcomes

**Function:** make Auri’s ability to solve business problems concrete.

This page primarily uses the learning from **Notion**: calm structure, ordered information, and focus on understanding.

Narrative rule:

```text
real pain → scenario or example → Auri solution → expected outcome → conversation
```

Current areas to address objectively:

- Business automations: inventory, sales, logistics, and repetitive tasks.
- Systems and ERPs: centralization, organization, scheduling, and operations; do not use “simple” as a positioning crutch.
- Digital positioning: presence on Google and Instagram, basic SEO, and brand clarity.

`AI applied to productivity` should remain a standalone offer only if it has a specific delivery, real case, and clear benefit. Otherwise, AI belongs as a tool inside the other areas.

Do not:

- List tools without an outcome.
- Use cards with vague language such as “innovative solutions.”
- Promise transformation without an application example.
- Display areas that Auri cannot yet operate.

### 7.4 Products — sell concrete transformation

**Function:** present digital assets as immediately applicable tools.

This page primarily absorbs the learning from **Meta**: the product needs visual presence, demonstrated use, and an objective CTA.

Narrative rule:

```text
asset preview → problem solved → transformation → what is included → CTA
```

For every future product, define:

- name;
- specific user;
- starting situation and desired situation;
- format: template, guide, spreadsheet, e-book, or system;
- visual preview or video;
- what is included;
- availability and CTA.

Do not:

- Display a grid of eight abstract products at once.
- Represent a product card only with an icon.
- Launch a product without a preview or explanation of use.
- Create a store effect before a real product exists.

### 7.5 Editorial — build authority

**Function:** demonstrate depth of thought and build a stronger relationship with the audience.

Rules:

- Address technology, discipline, business, and performance with substance and application.
- Prioritize reading over decoration.
- Keep categories short and useful.
- Every article should lead to a coherent next action: another piece of content, a future resource, or contact when appropriate.

### 7.6 Contact — reduce friction

**Function:** turn interest into a clear first conversation.

Rules:

- Explain which kinds of conversations Auri accepts.
- Use direct fields: name, email, phone, subject, and message.
- Explain honestly: Auri reviews the message and reaches out for a first conversation.
- Do not present WhatsApp or email as functional if they are still being defined.

## 8. Media, imagery, and motion

### Image direction

- Dark, mineral, controlled contrast, and muted gold.
- Body, training, work, screens, desks, process, tools, and urban environments may appear when they reinforce building and performance.
- An image must have context, texture, or use. Avoid generic corporate stock imagery.
- In Products, showing the digital asset open and applied is more valuable than an abstract illustration.

### Video direction

- Short, silent, or with movement that works without audio.
- Show interaction, flow, use, or material detail.
- Do not use video merely to create “vibe.”

### Motion and microinteractions

- Short transitions: `150ms–220ms`.
- Hover must reinforce action: slight elevation, border change, or a subtle glow.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Do not use continuous animation, autoplay carousels, or effects that hinder reading.

---

## 9. Mobile-first and accessibility

### Mobile is the baseline

- Design reading and action first at `375px`.
- Validate also at `768px`, laptop size, and `1280px`.
- Buttons and navigation items need a minimum `44px` touch target.
- Stack cards; do not compress content just to preserve a grid.
- Hero text must never be cropped, cramped, or reduced below comfortable reading size.
- Categories and horizontal navigation must provide obvious scroll behavior when necessary.

### Mandatory accessibility basics

- High contrast between parchment and dark background.
- Useful `alt` text for relevant images; decorative images should be ignored by screen readers.
- Visible gold focus states.
- Forms with labels, correct input types, and understandable error messages.
- Do not rely on color alone to communicate status.

## 10. References retained for future application

The references below are approved as **patterns to study**, not as direct copies or automatic changes across every page.

### 10.1 What to absorb from Notion

| Pattern | Future application at Auri | Where to apply |
|---|---|---|
| Quiet hierarchy and ordered information | Pain → solution → detail, with room to read. | Services, Editorial, and Contact. |
| One structural color | Gold as a signal of action, focus, and value. | The entire system. |
| Quiet chrome | Fewer borders, icons, and effects; more typography and breathing room. | Cards, forms, and navigation. |
| Documentary interface | Content feels clear, trustworthy, and applicable. | Editorial and product pages. |

### 10.2 What to absorb from Meta

| Pattern | Future application at Auri | Where to apply |
|---|---|---|
| Product merchandising | Large media, real preview, and desire to use it. | Products and product pages. |
| Two-CTA hierarchy | One primary action and one exploration route. | Hero, Products, and Services. |
| Demonstration before specification | Show use, then explain format and details. | Digital products and cases. |
| Visual launch focus | One core product can receive a dedicated composition. | Future drops and launches. |

### 10.3 Adaptation boundaries

Do not copy:

- Meta’s white canvas and cobalt blue.
- Hardware e-commerce language.
- Notion’s colorful stickers and light SaaS feel.
- A workspace, dashboard, or generic-platform interface.
- Any pattern that erases Auri’s dark, premium, editorial atmosphere.

## 11. Anti-rules

The site is outside the Auri direction when:

- every section uses a different visual style;
- a gradient, border, icon, and badge compete for attention on the same card;
- cards present services or products without a scenario, outcome, or use;
- there are too many CTAs for one decision;
- images are generic, context-free, or disconnected from the delivery;
- a page tries to look like a corporate startup before a product is validated;
- a new idea enters because it looks technological rather than because it solves a real pain;
- the page loses mobile readability to preserve a desktop layout;
- technology content feels like an AI promise rather than applied work;
- the brand feels like a coach, cold, or artificial.

3. Human review and approval.
4. Execute with `executing-plans` and checkpoints.
5. Validate mobile-first and review Brazilian Portuguese copy.
6. Run technical checks proportionate to risk.
7. Consolidate the commit on GitHub.

### Rule for new references

When an external reference inspires a change:

1. Identify the principle; do not copy the appearance.
2. Record the pattern in this section or in the implementation plan.
3. Explain which Auri problem it solves.
4. Check that it respects palette, typography, tone, and current stage.
5. Apply it first to the page where it has the clearest function.

## 13. Visual review checklist

Before approving a page change, validate:

### Brand

- [ ] Does it feel like Auri even without the logo?
- [ ] Does it maintain the dark, editorial, athletic, and technological atmosphere?
- [ ] Does gold have a function rather than serve as random decoration?
- [ ] Is the copy direct, real, and free of a coaching tone?

### Hierarchy and content

- [ ] Is the first message on the screen understandable within seconds?
- [ ] Is there one clear primary CTA?
- [ ] Does every card have a function and avoid repeating information?
- [ ] Does the product or service show use, scenario, or outcome?

### Interface

- [ ] Does typography establish hierarchy before borders, icons, and color?
- [ ] Is spacing consistent and does the page breathe?
- [ ] Are hover, focus, and active states clear?
- [ ] Are there no effects, gradients, or colors without a function?

### Mobile

- [ ] Does it work at 375px without overflow?
- [ ] Are CTAs and navigation easy to tap?
- [ ] Do images and text preserve readability and priority?
- [ ] Do cards stack without losing context?

## 15. How to update this document

Update this file only when a decision becomes a repeatable rule for the whole website or a family of pages.

Do not update it for isolated margin, copy, or color adjustments. In those cases, use the specific implementation plan.

When updating:

1. Increase the version.
2. Update the date.
3. Record the decision in the history.
4. Explain why the rule changed.
5. Ensure the change does not contradict Auri’s essence.
